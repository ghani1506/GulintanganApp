Use your own instrument

Load a recording of each gong and the app plays your real instrument. A note with no recording borrows the nearest one you loaded and shifts its pitch.

Fastest way: record just one gong. The app finds which note it is and builds every other note from it automatically.

Tap Record one note, then strike a gong within 3 seconds.

For the most realistic sound, record every gong:

  1. Record each gong struck once, about 2 seconds, in a quiet room with the phone about 30 cm away. Voice Memos or any recorder app works.
  2. Name the files by note, such as C5, C#5 (or Cs5), D5, Eb5. Then load them all at once and each goes to the right gong. Otherwise use Load on each row.
  3. Play with Sound set to My recordings. Recordings stay saved in this browser on this device.
  • Centre knob (pencu): tap it for the full, deep tone. Strike the shoulder for a brighter, shorter ring.
  • Several fingers work at the same time, so you can play the melody and the drone together.
  • Sound: modelled on caklempong brass kettle gongs, with a bright, clear and fairly short ring. Choose Damped for a crisp, stopped note.
  • No sound on iPhone? Turn the volume up and switch off Silent mode.
  • Computer keys: A S D F G H J K (natural), or A W S E D F T G Y H U J K (chromatic).

The Synth sound is generated in your browser to imitate caklempong gongs. Switch Sound to My recordings to hear your own instrument.
